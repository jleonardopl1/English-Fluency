# Curriculum & CEFR Path (A1 → C2)

A roadmap of what to focus on at each level, tuned for Brazilian learners aiming at native-like American English. Use it to (a) place the learner, (b) pick what to teach next, and (c) show them the path. Don't lecture CEFR jargon — use it behind the scenes.

The destination ("speak like a native American") sits at C1–C2 + strong pronunciation + cultural/idiomatic fluency. Progress isn't linear or uniform — a learner can be B2 in vocabulary but A2 in pronunciation. Track skills separately (see progress file).

## A1 — Beginner
**Can do:** introduce self, basic needs, simple present, very short exchanges.
**Focus:** core verbs (be, have, do, go, want, like, need, can); present simple; basic questions (what/where/when/who); numbers, time, days; ~500 high-frequency words; subject pronouns; a/an/the basics.
**Brazilian priorities:** drop "I'm with hunger" → "I'm hungry"; "I have 20 years" → "I'm 20"; don't add -ee to word endings; start tackling TH.
**Goal:** produce *any* output, build confidence, lots of speaking even if imperfect.

## A2 — Elementary
**Can do:** routine tasks, simple descriptions of background/environment, short social exchanges.
**Focus:** past simple (regular + common irregulars); future (will / going to); present continuous; comparatives/superlatives; countable/uncountable; common prepositions of time/place; ~1000–1500 words; basic phrasal verbs (get up, turn on, look for).
**Brazilian priorities:** third-person -s (he goes); "there is/are" vs "have"; make/do; -ed pronunciation; articles with general nouns.
**Goal:** hold a simple conversation on familiar topics; connect a few sentences.

## B1 — Intermediate (the big one)
**Can do:** handle most travel situations, describe experiences/events/dreams, give brief reasons and explanations, sustain a conversation.
**Focus:** present perfect (and vs simple past); since/for; first & second conditionals; relative clauses (who/which/that); reported speech basics; modal verbs (should, must, might, could); more phrasal verbs; ~2500 words; collocations.
**Brazilian priorities:** present perfect + since (huge); preposition collocations (depend on, good at, interested in); say/tell, miss/lose, borrow/lend; false friends; reducing literal-translation phrasing.
**Goal:** converse comfortably, express opinions with reasons, start sounding less "translated."

## B2 — Upper-Intermediate
**Can do:** interact with fluency and spontaneity, argue a viewpoint, understand complex text, discuss abstract topics.
**Focus:** all conditionals (incl. third & mixed); passive voice; perfect modals (should have done); advanced connectors (however, although, despite, whereas); wider phrasal verbs & idioms; register (formal vs casual); ~4000 words; nuance in word choice.
**Brazilian priorities:** article subtleties; advanced prepositions; naturalness over correctness; intonation & sentence stress; reductions (gonna/wanna) for natural speech.
**Goal:** fluent on most topics; errors mostly in nuance; can hold their own with natives.

## C1 — Advanced
**Can do:** use English flexibly and effectively for social/academic/professional purposes, express ideas fluently and precisely, understand demanding texts and implicit meaning.
**Focus:** idiomatic mastery; subtle register shifts; advanced collocations; hedging & softening; discourse markers; humor, sarcasm, cultural references; near-native pronunciation (schwa, linking, intonation); ~8000 words.
**Brazilian priorities:** the last 10% of accent (connected speech, intonation melody); idioms used naturally (not forced); cultural fluency (American humor, small talk, references); precision in synonyms.
**Goal:** sound highly competent and near-native; rare, minor errors.

## C2 — Mastery / Native-like
**Can do:** understand virtually everything; express precisely and spontaneously; nuance, irony, connotation under control.
**Focus:** polishing the final details — connotation, rhythm, idiomatic range, regional/cultural nuance, code-switching register effortlessly. At this point, "study" gives way to *immersion and use* — lots of authentic input (podcasts, shows, books) and real conversation.
**Brazilian priorities:** eliminating the last subtle L1 tells; sounding indistinguishable from a native in casual and professional contexts.
**Goal:** native-like fluency. Maintenance > instruction.

## Using this to plan
- Teach the **next layer** for the learner's level, not three levels ahead. i+1.
- Always blend in their **top recurring errors** (from the progress file) regardless of level.
- From B1 up, increasingly trade "correct" for "natural" — that's the path to native.
- Pronunciation work runs *in parallel* from day one; don't postpone it to C1.
