# Brazilian Portuguese → English Interference Patterns

**Your secret weapon.** Because the learner's L1 is Brazilian Portuguese, you can *predict* most of their mistakes. When they make one of these, correct it with confidence and the short reason — and connect it to the Portuguese cause when that helps it click. Promote frequent ones into the spaced-repetition queue.

Use this as a lookup, not a script. Don't dump the whole list on the learner.

## 1. Grammar interference

| Mistake (typical) | Correct | Why (PT cause) |
|---|---|---|
| "Have many people here." | "There are many people here." | PT *tem* = both *have* and *there is/are*. Brazilians overuse "have" for existence. |
| "He don't like it." / "She have a car." | "He doesn't like it." / "She has a car." | PT verbs don't add 3rd-person -s, so it gets dropped. The single most common error — drill it. |
| "I have 25 years." | "I'm 25 (years old)." | PT uses *ter* for age (*tenho 25 anos*). English uses *be*. |
| "I am agree with you." | "I agree with you." | *Agree* is already a verb in English; no "to be." |
| "The people is happy." / "Everybody are here." | "People are happy." / "Everybody is here." | *People* is plural; *everybody* is singular. PT *o povo* is singular, *todo mundo* feels plural. |
| "I live here since 2015." | "I've lived here since 2015." / "I've been living here since 2015." | PT present + *desde* → English needs present perfect. |
| "Yesterday I have gone." | "Yesterday I went." | A finished past time → simple past, not present perfect. |
| "I didn't went." | "I didn't go." | After *did*, use the base verb. |
| "I no like coffee." | "I don't like coffee." | English negates with *do/does + not*, not just "no." |
| "I don't know nothing." | "I don't know anything." / "I know nothing." | English avoids double negatives; PT allows them. |
| "Explain me this." | "Explain this to me." | *Explain*, *say*, *suggest*, *describe* take "to + person." |
| "How is called?" / "How do you call this?" | "What's it called?" / "What do you call this?" | English uses *what*, not *how*, for naming. |
| "I'm here since two hours." | "I've been here for two hours." | *for* + duration, *since* + start point — and present perfect. |
| "More better" / "More easy" | "Better" / "Easier" | No "more" with comparative/short adjectives. |
| "It's depend." | "It depends." | Don't drop the -s; don't add "be." |
| "I have to study, no?" | "I have to study, right? / don't I?" | PT tag *né?/não?* → English question tags or "right?". |

**Articles** (Portuguese uses *the* far more than English):
| Mistake | Correct | Note |
|---|---|---|
| "I love the music." (in general) | "I love music." | No article for general/uncountable concepts. |
| "The life is hard." | "Life is hard." | Same. |
| "The Brazil is big." | "Brazil is big." | No article before most country names. |
| "I go to the church on Sunday" (the activity) | "I go to church on Sundays." | No article for institutions as activities (church, school, work, bed, hospital). |
| "I'm doctor." | "I'm a doctor." | English needs *a/an* before singular professions. |

**Uncountable nouns Brazilians wrongly pluralize** (PT pluralizes them): *information(s)*, *advice(s)*, *knowledge(s)*, *furniture(s)*, *equipment(s)*, *feedback(s)*, *money(s)*, *research(es)*, *homework(s)*, *software(s)*. → "an information" becomes "a piece of information" / "some information."

## 2. Vocabulary & word-choice traps

| Mistake | Correct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Make a question." | "Ask a question." | *Fazer uma pergunta* → English uses *ask*. |
| "I won the bus." / "I lost the bus." | "I caught the bus." / "I missed the bus." | *Ganhar/perder* map differently. |
| "I will win a gift." | "I'll get/receive a gift." | *Ganhar* ≠ always *win*. |
| "Take a coffee." | "Have / grab a coffee." | *Tomar um café*. |
| "I have reason." | "I'm right." | *Ter razão* → *be right*. |
| "I pretend to travel." | "I plan/intend to travel." | *Pretender* = intend (false friend, see §3). |
| "Put attention." | "Pay attention." | Collocation: *pay* attention. |
| "Do a party." | "Throw / have a party." | *Fazer* maps to make/do/throw depending on context. |
| "Realize a dream." | "Achieve / fulfill a dream." | *Realizar* ≠ *realize* (false friend). |
| "Assist the movie." | "Watch the movie." | *Assistir* = watch (false friend). |
| "I have certain." | "I'm sure." | *Ter certeza* → *be sure*. |

**make vs do** (PT *fazer* = both). Rough rule: **do** = tasks/activities/work (do homework, do the dishes, do business, do a favor). **make** = create/produce/result (make a decision, make money, make a mistake, make dinner, make friends, make a difference). Teach by examples, not the rule alone.

**say vs tell:** *say* something (to someone); *tell* someone something. "He told me that…" / "He said that…" Never "He said me."

**speak vs talk:** mostly interchangeable; *speak* is slightly more formal / for languages ("I speak English"); *talk* is conversational ("let's talk").

## 3. False friends (falsos cognatos) — call these out proactively

| English word | Brazilians think it means | It actually means | Portuguese for what they meant |
|---|---|---|---|
| actually | atualmente (currently) | in fact / really | currently → *currently / nowadays* |
| pretend | pretender (intend) | fake / act as if | intend → *plan / intend* |
| realize | realizar (carry out) | become aware | carry out → *carry out / achieve* |
| assist | assistir (watch) | help | watch → *watch* |
| attend | atender (answer/serve) | be present at | answer → *answer / serve* |
| push | (looks like puxar) | empurrar | puxar → *pull* |
| pull | — | puxar | empurrar → *push* |
| library | livraria (bookstore) | biblioteca | bookstore → *bookstore* |
| parents | parentes (relatives) | pais (mother+father) | relatives → *relatives* |
| college | colégio (high school) | faculdade / university | high school → *high school* |
| comprehensive | compreensivo (understanding) | thorough/complete | understanding → *understanding* |
| sensible | sensível (sensitive) | reasonable | sensitive → *sensitive* |
| eventually | eventualmente (occasionally) | in the end / finally | occasionally → *occasionally* |
| fabric | fábrica (factory) | cloth/material | factory → *factory* |
| lecture | leitura (reading) | palestra / aula | reading → *reading* |
| novel | novela (soap opera) | romance (book) | soap opera → *soap opera / TV series* |
| legend | legenda (subtitle/caption) | lenda | subtitle → *subtitle* |
| policy | polícia (police) | política/diretriz | police → *police* |
| costume | costume (habit/custom) | fantasia (outfit) | habit → *custom / habit* |
| support | suportar (tolerate) | apoiar | tolerate → *tolerate / stand* |
| record (verb) | recordar (remember) | gravar | remember → *remember* |
| resume (verb) | resumir (summarize) | retomar | summarize → *summarize* |
| data | data (date) | dados | date → *date* |
| exquisite | esquisito (weird) | refined/beautiful | weird → *weird* |
| cigar | cigarro (cigarette) | charuto | cigarette → *cigarette* |
| mayor | maior (bigger) | prefeito | bigger → *bigger* |
| lunch | lanche (snack) | almoço | snack → *snack* |
| notice | notícia (news) | perceber / aviso | news → *news* |

## 4. "Estar com" / "ter" + noun → English "be" + adjective

Portuguese says *estar com / ter* + noun where English uses *be* + adjective. Very common beginner tell.

| Mistake | Correct |
|---|---|
| "I'm with hunger." / "I have hunger." | "I'm hungry." |
| "I'm with cold." | "I'm cold." |
| "I'm with fear." / "I have fear." | "I'm scared / afraid." |
| "I'm with sleep." | "I'm sleepy / tired." |
| "I'm with thirst." | "I'm thirsty." |
| "I'm with hurry." | "I'm in a hurry." |
| "I have luck." | "I'm lucky." |
| "I have sure." | "I'm sure." |

## 5. Preposition map (the hardest area — drill heavily)

| Wrong | Right | Phrase |
|---|---|---|
| depend of | depend **on** | "It depends on you." |
| married with | married **to** | "She's married to him." |
| good in | good **at** | "I'm good at math." |
| interested on | interested **in** | "interested in music" |
| arrive to/in (a place) | arrive **at** (place) / **in** (city/country) | "arrive at the office / in Brazil" |
| think **in** | think **about/of** | "think about it" |
| afraid from | afraid **of** | "afraid of dogs" |
| listen (no prep) | listen **to** | "listen to me" |
| wait (no prep) | wait **for** | "wait for me" |
| in weekend | **on** the weekend (US) | "on the weekend" |
| in Monday | **on** Monday | days take *on* |
| congratulations **for** | congratulations **on** | "congrats on the job" |
| different of/than | different **from** (US standard) | "different from mine" |

Time prepositions quick rule: **at** + clock time/night ("at 5", "at night") · **on** + days/dates ("on Monday", "on July 4th") · **in** + months/years/periods ("in May", "in 2026", "in the morning").

## 6. Verbs Brazilians confuse

- **borrow vs lend** (*emprestar* is both): you *borrow* FROM someone (take); you *lend* TO someone (give). "Can I borrow your pen?" / "I'll lend you my pen."
- **win vs earn vs gain** (*ganhar*): *win* a game/prize; *earn* money/respect (through work); *gain* weight/experience.
- **miss vs lose** (*perder*): *miss* a bus/flight/chance/person; *lose* keys/a game/weight.
- **hear vs listen**: *hear* = passive (it reaches your ears); *listen* = active (you pay attention).
- **see vs watch vs look**: *see* (perceive), *watch* (follow over time: a movie, TV), *look at* (direct your eyes).
- **come vs go / bring vs take**: *come/bring* = toward here; *go/take* = away from here.
- **remember vs remind**: *remember* = recall yourself; *remind* = make someone else recall ("remind me to…").
- **fun vs funny**: *fun* = enjoyable; *funny* = makes you laugh (or strange). "The party was fun" ≠ "The party was funny."

## 7. Naturalness & register

- Brazilians often translate idioms literally ("it's not my beach" 😄). Teach the real English equivalent.
- Over-formality: textbook English ("I would like to inquire…") in casual situations. Teach contractions (I'm, don't, gonna, wanna), and that "How are you?" usually wants "Good, you?" not a health report.
- **Connectors** that sound translated: "actually" misused (§3), "in this way", "make a confusion". Replace with native discourse markers: *so, well, anyway, by the way, I mean, you know, kind of, basically*.
- Phrasal verbs are the fluency frontier for Brazilians (Portuguese has fewer): *figure out, come up with, run into, get along with, look forward to, give up, put off, hang out, work out, end up*. Introduce a few at a time in context.
- Politeness softeners Americans use constantly: "Could you…?", "Do you mind…?", "I was wondering if…", "Would it be possible…?", "kind of / sort of", "maybe we could…". Direct translations from PT can sound blunt.

## How to use this in feedback

When the learner makes one of these, the correction block reason can name the cause briefly:
- `*[Grammar]* PT uses "tem" for both "have" and "there is" — in English, existence = "there is/are".`
- `*[False friend]* "actually" means "in fact", not "currently" (atualmente).`
- `*[Preposition]* "depend" always takes "on" in English.`

Connecting the error to Portuguese makes it memorable. But keep it to one line — don't lecture.
