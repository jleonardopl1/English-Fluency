# Correction Engine — how to give feedback that sticks

This file is the *how* of corrections. The block format itself is defined in `SKILL.md`; this expands on judgment.

## The 3-step correction

For each error worth fixing:
1. **Show the contrast:** `❌ ~~wrong~~ → ✅ correct`
2. **Tag + one-line why:** `*[Category]* short reason` (connect to Portuguese when it helps — see `pt-br-interference.md`).
3. **Optionally, a micro-rep:** for a high-value or recurring error, ask them to use it correctly right now: "Try a sentence using *depend on*."

Never just give the answer for something the learner could produce themselves — make them *retrieve* it (active recall).

## What to correct (prioritization)

You can't fix everything at once. Rank like this and take the top 3–4:
1. **Meaning-blocking errors** — the listener wouldn't understand or would misunderstand. Always fix.
2. **Recurring patterns** — errors you've seen before from this learner. Fix and flag (`🔁 Recurring`).
3. **Level-appropriate targets** — the grammar/vocab they're currently working on.
4. **Naturalness** — grammatically fine but un-American phrasing. Fix only when the learner is ready (B1+), and frame as "level up," not "wrong."
5. **Minor slips** — let small stuff slide in conversation with a quick "(a couple of tiny things I'll skip for now)". Catch them later in `/review`.

In `/drill` and `/test`, be more thorough — accuracy is the point there. In `/chat` and `/roleplay`, protect the flow.

## Severity cues (optional, helpful for beginners)

- 🔴 **Critical:** breaks communication (wrong verb, no negation, scrambled word order).
- 🟡 **Moderate:** noticeable but understandable (tense, preposition, article).
- 🟢 **Minor:** spelling, small slips. Low priority in conversation.

## "Level up" rephrases (push toward native)

When a learner's sentence is correct, occasionally offer a more native version and explain the upgrade:

> ⭐ Clean! Here's how a native might say it for extra polish:
> "I'm gonna grab lunch real quick" instead of "I will eat lunch quickly" — *gonna, grab, real quick* sound natural and casual.

Use this to teach contractions, phrasal verbs, collocations, and rhythm — the things that separate "correct" from "native."

## Handling "perfect" turns

Never skip the feedback block. If the English is genuinely clean:
- `⭐ Clean! Nice work.` and either (a) a "level up" rephrase to stretch them, or (b) a quick "phrase of the day"/idiom related to the topic. Variety keeps it fresh.

## Repetition tracking

When the same error appears again:
- Name the pattern out loud and count it: "That's the 3rd time the **3rd-person -s** slipped."
- Promote it to a *focus pattern* in the progress file and the review queue (`spaced-repetition.md`).
- Once they get it right several times in a row, celebrate: "You've now used it correctly 4 times running — I think we can retire this one. 🎉"

## Teaching new vocabulary/idioms

When you introduce a word, phrase, idiom, or phrasal verb:
- Give **meaning + one natural example sentence** + (for beginners) the Portuguese gloss.
- Mark stress for multi-syllable words (e.g. *com·FOR·ta·ble*).
- Add it to the review queue so it comes back.
- Prefer teaching *in context* (from something the learner just said) over random word lists.

## Rotating "Learn something new" (optional, end of a turn or session)

Pick ONE, rotating across turns so it doesn't get repetitive:
- **Phrase of the day** — a useful idiom/collocation tied to the topic.
- **Grammar tip** — a short rule targeting an error the learner tends to make.
- **Level up** — rephrase one of their correct sentences more natively.
- **Culture note** — a small American-culture/usage insight (small talk norms, "how's it going?", tipping vocab, etc.).
